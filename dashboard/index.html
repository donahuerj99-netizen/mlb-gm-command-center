METHODOLOGY & ARCHETYPES
How WAR projections are calculated and what each archetype means
DATA FOUNDATION

Trained on 18,056 player-seasons spanning 2000-2025 from Baseball Reference, covering 2,184 position players and 2,504 pitchers. Includes batting, advanced, and fielding statistics.

PREDICTION ENSEMBLE

Three models averaged: Gradient Boosting, Random Forest, and Ridge Regression. GroupKFold cross-validation prevents data leakage across player careers.

CONFIDENCE INTERVALS

Bootstrap sampling (50 iterations) generates P10/P50/P90 bands. Injury adjustments reduce projected WAR by archetype-specific risk factors by age band.

CONTRACT VALUATION

Fair AAV = injury-adjusted WAR x $10.5M/WAR x 5% annual inflation, discounted at 5%/year. SIGN = surplus value. CAUTION = slight overpay. AVOID = significant overpay.

KEY FEATURES

Top predictors: current WAR, 3-year WAR average, WAR trajectory, age, archetype, wRC+, FIP (pitchers), Rtot/Rdrs defensive runs saved, sprint speed. Recent seasons weighted 3x vs older data.

ELITE CLASS
★
Franchise Cornerstone

Peak WAR 5+, wRC+ 125+. Sustained elite performance across multiple seasons. The foundation of championship rosters.

e.g. Mike Trout, Albert Pujols, Aaron Judge
★
Two-Way Superstar

Elite offense AND elite defense. The rarest profile in baseball — combines 4+ WAR production with top-tier Def ratings and speed.

e.g. Mookie Betts, Mike Trout, Andrew Jones
POWER CLASS
⚡
Pure Power Masher

Elite ISO with controlled strikeout rates. Big raw power but makes enough contact to be consistently dangerous. Classic middle-of-the-order bat.

e.g. David Ortiz, Miguel Cabrera, Ryan Howard
⚡
High-K Power Threat

Big power, high ISO, elevated strikeout rates. Boom-or-bust profiles. Best on shorter contracts — high variance in outcomes.

e.g. Kyle Schwarber, Joey Gallo, Mark Reynolds
CONTACT & OBP CLASS
◈
Elite Contact Hitter

Exceptionally low strikeout rates with solid offensive production. Hard to put out, consistently puts the ball in play. Ages well.

e.g. Tony Gwynn Jr., Ichiro, Luis Arraez
◉
Patient OBP Machine

Elite walk rates and on-base skills. Undervalued by traditional metrics. Plate discipline persists with age — good long-term value.

e.g. Joey Votto, Carlos Santana, Adam Dunn
SPEED & DEFENSE CLASS
▶
Speedy Slap Hitter

Speed-first, low ISO, elite baserunning. Decline sharply as speed fades. Avoid long deals past age 30.

e.g. Billy Hamilton, Dee Gordon
⬡
Glove-First Defender

Elite defense drives value with below-average offense. Ideal up-the-middle. Value depends on defensive durability.

e.g. Andrelton Simmons, Yadier Molina
⬟
Two-Way Threat

Solid defense, above-average speed, and consistent offensive production. All-around contributors who make teams better in multiple ways.

e.g. Lorenzo Cain, Carlos Beltran, Cody Bellinger
REGULAR & DEPTH CLASS
▣
All-Around Regular

Average to above-average across all dimensions. Reliable everyday players who don't have a glaring weakness. Good lineup depth.

e.g. Typical #4-5 hitter
▢
Solid Contributor

Below-average regulars who provide roster stability. Good on short deals, not worth long-term commitments.

e.g. Typical #6-7 hitter
○
Bench/Utility Role

Below-average WAR producers. Value comes from positional flexibility. Avoid multi-year deals.

e.g. Typical bench player
★
Ace / Frontline Starter

Sub-2.75 FIP, high strikeout rates, 5+ WAR seasons. Franchise-defining arms challenging for Cy Young awards.

e.g. Tarik Skubal, Paul Skenes, Jacob deGrom
⚡
Power Arm Starter

High-strikeout starters with FIP under 3.20. Capable of ace-level seasons with high upside and durability.

e.g. Garrett Crochet, Gerrit Cole
◈
No. 2 / Quality Starter

Reliable starters with FIP 3.20-3.60. Consistent 3-4 WAR contributors who eat innings every fifth day.

e.g. Zack Wheeler, Logan Webb
▣
Mid-Rotation Starter

Average starters with FIP 3.60-4.20. Provide rotation stability. Value comes from durability and consistency.

e.g. Typical No. 3-4 starter
○
Back-End Starter

Below-average starters providing depth. Best suited for 5th starter roles or emergency callups.

e.g. Typical No. 5 starter
▲
Elite Closer / Dominant RP

Sub-2.50 FIP with elite strikeout rates. Premium high-leverage arms. Top free agent targets in the bullpen market.

e.g. Josh Hader, Emmanuel Clase
◉
High-Leverage Setup Man

FIP under 3.20, strong strikeout rates. Handle 7th-8th innings. Often undervalued relative to closers.

e.g. Ryan Pressly, Paul Sewald
▷
Solid Middle Reliever

Average bullpen arms eating innings in non-critical spots. FIP 3.20-3.80. Essential roster depth pieces.

e.g. Typical middle reliever
